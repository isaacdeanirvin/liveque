LiveQue business cards

White ink on black. Glen is deliverable 1, Isaac is deliverable 2. Shown at 2× actual size.

The card is #080808. That is sampled straight out of index.html line 11, not eyeballed. It is a hair off pure black, which is why the card reads slightly softer than a CMYK rich black next to it.

Worth knowing before you order: you cannot specify a hex on dyed-through stock. Colorplan Ebony is a colour the paper already is. It is the closest thing to #080808 Jukebox sells with matched edges, but nobody is colour-matching it to your site. The alternative is printing a rich black build on white stock, which hits the number more exactly and then shows a white edge on every cut and every scuff. Ebony is the right call.
Foil is out, and it was the QR that killed it. ISO/IEC 18004 Annex K says specular reflection off a glossy surface interferes with reading, and matt is what scanners want. Foil is polished metal. On Premium Black there is no ink at all, only foil, so there is nothing else to build a QR from and somebody has to type the URL. Your call to go white on black also saves about $836 CAD across both brothers.

Two things before this prints

1. The QR is a placeholder pattern, not a working code. It is the right size and quiet zone, but the modules are decorative. It needs replacing with real vector art pointing at getliveque.com, and it needs a test scan off an actual printed proof, not a screen.

2. This HTML is the design, not the production file. A browser export gives raster or badly structured vector, which shows immediately on something read at arm's length. It also needs a licensed typeface chosen: the app runs on Segoe UI, which is Microsoft-licensed and not yours to embed in a print file.
