LiveQue business cards — final

Glen is deliverable 1, Isaac is deliverable 2. Shown at 2× actual size. Two versions, because foil turns out to carry a cost beyond money.

You cannot make a reliable QR code out of foil. ISO/IEC 18004 Annex K says it directly: specular reflection off a glossy surface interferes with reading, and matt surfaces are what scanners want. Foil is polished metal. A silver foil QR throws light straight back at the camera and reads inconsistently depending on how the card is tilted.

On Premium Black there is no ink, only foil, so there is nothing else to build a QR from. The foil version below has no QR. Someone has to type the URL.
Why silver. Measured with CIEDE2000 against the site palette, metallic silver is the closest foil to #4ecdc4 of anything Jukebox showed on black (ΔE 22.8), and it sits within 4 lightness points of the teal, so it carries the same visual weight on the card that teal carries on the site.

Being straight about the number though: ΔE over about 5 reads as a clearly different colour, so 22.8 is not a match. No metal can match teal, because metals are neutral and teal is chromatic. Silver simply has no competing hue, which is what makes it the least wrong. For contrast, gold foil matches your #ffd700 at ΔE 9.7 — your palette does contain a foil-matchable colour, it just is not the teal.

Holographic Silver is the wildcard. It measures the same as plain silver (ΔE 23.7) but that average is misleading: holographic foil diffracts across the spectrum, so it actually flashes cyan and teal as the card moves. It is the only foil that will ever genuinely show your brand colour. Worth asking about for the LiveQue mark alone.
Now confirmed on Jukebox's own page: Colorplan is "through-dyed pulp… with color-matched edges." No white core, guaranteed. That was the one thing the research could not verify earlier.
