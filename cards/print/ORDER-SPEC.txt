===========================================================================
DO NOT ADD SOFT TOUCH. DO NOT ADD LAMINATE.
===========================================================================

Two reasons, and the first one is not a matter of taste.

1. Taghleef/Derprosa invented the soft-touch film category. Their standard
   soft-touch datasheet never mentions fingerprints. Their separate
   Pro-Shield product advertises "remarkable anti-fingerprints properties"
   and is "recommended for all types of DARK COLOR BACKGROUNDS that make
   smudges more visible." That is the manufacturer conceding the defect in
   order to sell the fix. On a black card, soft touch is the worst
   available choice.

2. UNCOATED TAKES A PEN. Laminate does not. Musicians write on each other's
   cards: set times, venue names, "call me about Tuesday." A laminated card
   cannot be written on. This is a real advantage of uncoated Colorplan for
   your specific use, and no vendor will ever mention it.

Uncoated also hides scuffs better than matte laminate, and matte surfaces
read as more sincere than gloss in the only finish comparison with real
peer-reviewed evidence behind it (Chen 2021, J. Product & Brand Management
30(5): matte scored 5.01 vs gloss 3.68 on brand sincerity).

===========================================================================
DO NOT PAY FOR THICKNESS BEYOND 18PT
===========================================================================

The "heavier stock feels more premium" idea rests on the weight-as-
importance literature, and that literature has collapsed. Rabelo et al.
2015 (PLOS ONE 10(8)) meta-analysed 25 published weight-importance effects:
the naive estimate was d=0.57, but the homogeneity test came back
p=0.999993, which is impossibly uniform, and the publication-bias-corrected
estimate is NEGATIVE. Three of their own experiments were null.

No study has ever tested paper caliper against perceived quality. 18pt is a
cheap upgrade over 16pt and worth taking. Past that you are paying real
money for a difference nobody has shown anyone notices.

Also skip letterpress, foil, emboss and painted edges unless you simply
want them. There is no peer-reviewed evidence that any of them raise
perceived value. Every claim to the contrary traces to a printer selling
that finish. Deboss and emboss are also invisible in a dark bar, which is
where this card gets handed over.

===========================================================================
THE "88% OF CARDS ARE THROWN OUT IN A WEEK" STAT IS FAKE
===========================================================================

Traced to the end: Adobe blog (2016) credited it to SilkCards, a business
card printing company, whose infographic has no sources section, no links,
and no citation for any of its eight statistics. The chain terminates in a
printer's marketing graphic.

Same for "72% judge a company by its business card" (traces to Statistic
Brain, the outfit behind the debunked 8-second attention span, which the
BBC could not get to substantiate anything) and "68% perceive brands as
more premium with heavier stock" (attributed to a "Paper Industry
Association" that could not be confirmed to exist).

There is NO verifiable data on business card retention. Anyone quoting you
a number is repeating a printer's marketing.

What IS evidenced, and it is free: physically handing someone an object and
letting them hold it raises what they think it is worth by 37% to 61%
(Wolf/Arkes/Muhanna 2008; Bushong/King/Camerer/Rangel 2010, American
Economic Review 100(4)). And a card that feels UNPLEASANT actively destroys
that gain rather than being neutral (Peck & Shu 2009, JCR 36(3), Study 4).

The biggest wins here cost nothing. The expensive upgrades have no
evidence behind them at all.

Foil was considered and rejected: foil cannot carry a scannable QR code
(ISO/IEC 18004 Annex K - specular reflection off polished metal interferes
with reading), and it cost about $836 CAD more across both brothers.

THE QR CODE - READ THIS
===========================================================================

This is a REAL, WORKING QR CODE. Not a placeholder.

  Encodes:           https://getliveque.com
  Error correction:  H (30% recoverable - survives scuffs and dark bars)
  Version:           3  (29 x 29 modules)
  Printed size:      0.87 in  (spec floor is 0.8 in)
  Quiet zone:        full 4 modules on all sides
  Verified:          decoded out of the finished card art, and still decoded
                     when downscaled to 400px wide, far below what any phone
                     camera captures

TELL JUKEBOX: "Please double-hit the white ink."
  This is the single most important thing to say when you order.

  Digital white on dark stock commonly lands at only 60-75% opacity.
  Decoder testing shows the killer is not low contrast, it is PATCHINESS:
  an opaque but mottled white patch dropped to an 80% decode rate, and
  moderate opacity with heavy mottle failed 100% of the time. A double hit
  is what makes the white solid rather than blotchy.

  Do NOT let anyone letterpress the white. A practitioner on Briar Press
  reports needing SEVEN layers in perfect register to get opaque white
  letterpress, and another says it simply does not work. Foil, screen
  print, or a double-hit digital white are the routes that give real
  opacity. Letterpress would also crush the fine QR detail.

POLARITY IS ALREADY CORRECT. The white panel prints in white ink and the
dark modules are simply an absence of ink, letting the Ebony show through.
That gives standard dark-on-light, which every scanner handles. An
inverted QR (light modules on a dark field) is the common failure and
this design avoids it.

STILL DO THIS: when the printed proof arrives, scan it with an actual phone
before approving the full run. Screen tests are not print tests.
