# Landing page copy: what the evidence actually says

Four research passes, July 2026. Every source below was fetched and read. Where a
number could not be verified from a primary source it is marked. Where no evidence
exists, that is stated rather than filled in.

Read this before changing landing copy. Several things "everybody knows" about
conversion copywriting turn out to trace back to nothing.

---

## 0. The finding that governs everything else

Message design choices produce small and directionally unreliable effects.

O'Keefe & Hoeken (2021), *Frontiers in Psychology* 12:664160, meta-analysis of
**1,149 studies** across 30 message-design variations. Median mean effect
**r = 0.10**. Critically, **93% of prediction intervals included both positive and
negative values.** Picking the on-average-better option does not reliably help in
any specific application.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8275937/

Corroborated by industry: Microsoft reports only about **1/3 of ideas improve the
metric they targeted**; Amazon's success rate is below 50%; QualPro tested 150,000
ideas over 22 years and found **75% had no impact or hurt**.
https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/ExPThinkWeek2009Public.pdf

**Consequence for us:** at a 5% signup rate, detecting a +10% relative lift at 80%
power needs roughly **31,000 visitors per arm**. We will never have that traffic.
We cannot A/B our way to a hero section. Pick defensible defaults, ship, and spend
the time on distribution to musicians instead.

## 1. What gigging musicians actually fear (this one changed the page)

~205 posts across 6 threads on TalkBass, Acoustic Guitar Forum and TDPRI. Forum
anecdote, labelled as such, and skewed older and more US-cover-band than a Reddit
sample would be (Reddit was unreachable).

| Stated reason for declining a request | Approx. posts |
|---|---|
| **"We don't know it / not in our repertoire"** | **~40** |
| Setlist flow, "human jukebox", artistic control | ~10 |
| Genre mismatch | ~8 |
| Band coordination | ~6 |

**The dominant fear is competence and social awkwardness, not sovereignty.** Players
are afraid of having to say "I don't know that one" in front of a room. They are not
mainly afraid of losing control of the setlist.

Worse for the old framing: **musicians who take a hard anti-request line get mocked
by other musicians.** A player quoting Dweezil Zappa's "this is why we have a set
list folks" drew the reply "I guess Dweezil has inherited some of his dad's old
penchant for snarky behaviors." On the Acoustic Guitar Forum, a poster who said his
band would "never take requests" was told twice that this was "nonsense."
https://www.talkbass.com/threads/how-do-you-shoot-down-song-requests.1448282/page-3
https://www.acousticguitarforum.com/forums/showthread.php?t=214451

**So "No, they can't make you play Wonderwall" was answering the wrong question and
risked reading as the attitude their peers mock.** Replaced with "They can only ask
for songs you already play," which addresses the ~40-post fear instead of the
~10-post one and is a dignity promise rather than a defiance promise.

Drunk and persistent requesters are real: ~9 independent mentions, including a
patron pulling a player's hair back mid-song over a Jimmy Buffett request.

**Latent demand, unprompted:** 18 independent posts describe charging for requests
("Sure, we will play that if you put a $20 in the tip jar"), and 6 describe inventing
the repertoire-bounded mechanic by hand ("We hand them our master song list and say,
pick any song on there, drop a tip in the bucket and it's next"). Musicians have
already invented LiveQue on paper.

## 2. Where the objection belongs on the page

O'Keefe (1999), "How to handle opposing arguments in persuasive messages," meta-analysis,
**k = 107, N = 20,111**. https://dokeefe.net/pub/OKeefe99AICA.pdf

- Refutational two-sided: **r = .077**
- Nonrefutational two-sided (hedging, conceding): **r = −.049**, reliably negative
- In advertising specifically: credibility **r = .148** (significant), persuasion
  **r = .002** (null)

Order effects on credibility:
- support-then-oppose **r = .096** (significant)
- interwoven **r = .141** (significant)
- **oppose-then-support r = .014 (not significant)**

**The credibility payoff disappears when the objection leads.** The song-list section
now sits after How It Works and What It Costs, not above them.

Never soften it into a concession. Nonrefutational acknowledgment carries a reliably
negative effect.

**Honest gap:** whether raising an objection the reader did not already hold *creates*
the doubt is **unresolved**. O'Keefe's low-counterargument-availability cell is
k = 7, r = .051, 95% CI [−.240, .342]. No evidence of harm, no evidence of benefit.
The capability framing was chosen because it wins on either branch.

Inoculation theory does not license pre-empting an objection the audience lacks.
Classic inoculation is *prophylactic*, protecting a position already held; applying it
otherwise is a recent theoretical departure (Compton 2020, *Communication Theory* 30(3),
330–343). Banas & Rains (2010) meta-analysis, 54 cases, is real, but the widely
circulated **d = 0.43** figure could not be verified from any primary source and must
not be used.

## 3. Pricing transparency

Mohan, Buell & John (2020), "Lifting the Veil: The Benefits of Cost Transparency,"
*Marketing Science* 39(6):1105–1121. **Preregistered field experiment, N = 9,227**
plus 5 online replications. Voluntary cost disclosure made customers **21.1% more
likely to buy** (2.3% → 2.8%, p = .04 one-tailed). Mechanism is trust. **Boundary
condition: the effect appears only when disclosure is voluntary, not compelled.**
https://ximarketing.github.io/class/papers/transparency.pdf

The mirror image: Blake, Moshary, Sweeney & Tadelis (2021), *Marketing Science* /
NBER WP 25186. **Millions of StubHub users randomized.** Making the full price salient
*reduced* purchases; quality effects account for at least 28% of the revenue decline.
https://www.nber.org/papers/w25186

These do not conflict. Hiding fees earns more *if you have fees to hide*, which is why
fee-taking competitors bury their take rate. We have none, so stating it plainly is a
real differentiator rather than a cost.

### But "0% platform fee" is not by itself a differentiator

Four of five closest competitors already claim some version of free, then charge the
**fan**:

| Product | Headline | Actual fee |
|---|---|---|
| NoSongRequests | "The Only QR Code You Will Ever Need" | Free tier has no tips; Pro $10/mo |
| mySet | "Song Requests Made Easy" | "No hidden fees or commissions", fan pays $1.50 + processing |
| Human Jukebox | "Turn Song Requests Into Tips" | 7% platform fee + Stripe |
| Hear Your Song Now | "The easiest way for fans to request songs and tip musicians" | 10% + $0.30 charged to the fan |
| Song Request Live | "Built for real performers. Real crowds. Real control." | $0–3/mo, never touches the money |

**The claim only we can make: nobody pays a platform fee, not the performer and not
the person tipping.** A $5 tip costs the crowd $5. That is checkable and unmatched.

Setlist control is **table stakes, not a wedge**. NoSongRequests already runs "No
Surprise Requests. Only Songs You Actually Play." Our real gap is that every
competitor at scale is DJ-first; we are the only one aimed at live instrument-playing
cover musicians.

## 4. Tip anchors (product change, not copy)

Elkins & Fry (2026), "QR code-enabled tips to street performers at the Edinburgh
Fringe Festival," *Journal of Cultural Economics* 50:105–128. Peer-reviewed, open
access. **1,587 performances, 82 performers.** Average tip £5.01.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10824-025-09559-9

**The centre/default anchor is the dominant lever on tip size.** Versus a £3 reference:
£5 **+0.42\***, £10 **+0.85\*\*\***, £15 **+1.13\*\*\***, £20 **+1.16\*\*\***.
No backlash from high defaults in this spontaneous-giving context. No compromise
effect: only the anchor mattered.

**Our $5 default sits at the bottom of the tested range.** Worth revisiting.

Also from the same paper: solo performers got significantly more scans than groups;
**more payment options reduced scans** while raising amounts, so keep the flow simple;
599 of 1,587 performances got zero scans.

### One structural warning

Warren, Hanson & Yuan (2020), *Journal of Service Research*: requesting a tip
**before** rather than after a service produces smaller tips, lower return intentions
and worse ratings, driven by inferred manipulative intent. **"Pay to request a song"
is structurally a pre-service ask.**

Mitigations with direct evidential support:
- Let people change the amount afterwards (Warren & Hanson 2025, *JBR* 186)
- Keep no-tip and custom options visible
- Keep selection private on the punter's own phone. Same paper: reduced tipping
  privacy lowers repatronage. **A QR flow on your own phone is more private than a
  passed jar, which is a genuine peer-reviewed advantage over the physical tip jar.**

## 5. Cash decline: our hero line is supported, but do not say cash is dead

Federal Reserve Diary of Consumer Payment Choice. Cash as share of payments:
**31% (2016) → 26% (2019) → 19% (2020) → 16% (2023) → 14% (2024/25)**. Among
18–24s, cash is **6%** of payments and phones are **45%**.
https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/news/research/2025-diary-of-consumer-payment-choice.pdf

**Counterweight, stated honestly:** the Fed says there may be a floor, cash payments
have been flat at 6–7/month since 2021, and **cash is most used for payments under
$25**, which is exactly the tip band. ECB SPACE 2024 has euro-area bars and cafés
still **57% cash**. Bank of Canada and RBA both show cash *rising* slightly.

Near-verbatim corroboration of our headline, subway musician Natalia Paruz:
"I love your music, I want to give, but I'm sorry, I don't carry cash."
https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/street-performers-rely-apps-venmo-cash-fades-rcna223138

**Do not assume cashless raises tipping.** Kakkar & Li (2022), *JBEE* 97, real
restaurant data: probability of tipping is **significantly higher with cash**, because
cash tipping is publicly observable.

## 6. FTC exposure on earnings claims

This is the reason the page describes *our* motivation rather than promising *your*
income.

- FTC v. Uber (2017), **$20M settlement**, overstated driver earnings
- FTC/DOJ v. Lyft (Oct 2024), **$2.1M penalty**; advertised rates drawn from the top
  20% of earners and silently included tips
- FTC Notice of Penalty Offenses Concerning Money-Making Opportunities (2021) went to
  1,100+ companies including DoorDash, Lyft and Uber. Exposure up to **$43,792 per
  violation**, and **disclaimers are not a defence if the net impression misleads**

Earnings claims must be substantiated on **typical** results, not best users, and tips
cannot be silently folded in. Feature framing carries no equivalent exposure.

Also relevant: Miller & Ratner (1998), *JPSP* 74(1) — people **systematically
overestimate how much money motivates others**. A founder predicting "musicians will
respond to 'earn more'" is exactly the population making that error. Pew (2021,
n=10,348) on why people do gig work: extra money 56%, cover income gaps 52%,
**control own schedule 49%**.

## 7. Folklore. Do not cite any of this.

Traced to the source and found empty:

1. **"Start my" beats "Start your" by 90%.** Origin found in archive: Aagaard, March
   2013. **No sample size, no significance stated.** The metric was click-through to a
   payment page, not signups. The same author wrote the **exact opposite** five months
   earlier ("the second person 'Your' has consistently outperformed 'My'"). The same
   90% is attached to at least three different button pairs across sources. Unbounce
   cites it not to its own data but to a third-party blog that does not contain the
   claim.
2. **Any ranking of headline archetypes** (benefit vs problem vs testimonial vs
   category). No controlled study exists.
3. **"Headlines with numbers get 36% higher CTR."** The 36% appears in a *reader
   comment* on the cited page, not the article.
4. **Claude Hopkins' "33% more light" and "52.7% vs over 50%."** Checked against the
   1923 primary text. Those strings **do not appear in the book at all.** Invented.
5. **"5x as many people read the headline as read the body."** A 1963 advertising-man
   assertion, not a study.
6. **"Users decide in 8 seconds."** Not found in NN/g's own research.
7. **Social-proof conversion stats** ("testimonials +34%", "video +80%"). Textbook
   citation laundering; every chain ends at another blog.
8. **"Long copy for expensive products, short for free offers."** No underlying study.
9. **"Digital tipping increases tips 15–30% vs cash jars."** No primary source exists
   for any version of this. Treat as fabricated.
10. **CTA verb tests generally.** The only peer-reviewed study isolating CTA verb form
    (Bartoš & Habarta 2019) returned a **null result** on every comparison.

**Base rate worth remembering:** GoodUI's own database reports 634 tests — 165 winning,
43 losing, **426 statistically insignificant**. Roughly two-thirds of real UI tests
find nothing. The blog literature consists almost entirely of the winners.

## 8. What is weakly supported but harmless

- **Concrete language.** Packard & Berger (2021), *JCR* 47(5):787–806. One SD more
  concreteness → +8.9% satisfaction, +13–30% 90-day spend. Real field data, but
  **correlational** and from customer-service dialogue, not web copy. Transfer is an
  extrapolation.
- **Short page.** Unbounce's 41,000-page dataset puts 250–725 words best for SaaS.
  **Observational, no controls**, heavily confounded with industry. Our page is 386
  words, which costs nothing either way.
- **Above the fold.** NN/g **revised its own 80.3% figure down to 57%** in 2018 (120
  participants, vs 21 in 2010). Anyone citing 80% today is citing a retired number.
  74% of viewing time is in the first two screenfuls.
- **Round numbers.** Backus, Blake & Tadelis (2019), *JPE* 127(4), **10.5M eBay
  listings**: round prices get offers 8–12% lower but are **15–25% more likely to
  sell**. Reiley & Samek, randomized field experiment, N≈10,500: dropping a suggested
  amount from $100 to $95 **cut gifts above $90 by over 30%**. Keep tip presets round.

## Gaps nobody can close

- No research exists on landing pages aimed at musicians or individual creators.
- No published test of earnings framing vs feature framing for gig-worker tools. Zero.
- No controlled comparison of a digital tip prompt against a physical tip jar.
- No research on song-request tipping specifically.
- Reddit was unreachable, so the musician sample skews older and more cover-band.
