The part every musician asks about first

You stay in control of the setlist.

Requests land in a queue on your phone. You decide what gets played, what gets skipped, and what never sees the light of day. Nobody can buy their way onto your stage — a tip moves a request up the list, and that's all it does.

How a song gets requested

One QR code on the table.

1
Your crowd

They scan the code and see your songs

Your actual library, searchable. Their browser opens — there's nothing to install, no account to make.

2
Your crowd

They request one — free, or with a tip

Apple Pay, Google Pay, a card, or your Venmo. A tip marks the request Priority so it sits at the top of your queue.

3
You

It appears on your phone, mid-set

Tipped requests jump to the top with the amount attached. Tap Play when you start it, Done when you finish. Silent — no dings while you're on stage.

4
You

The money's already yours

Tips go to your own Stripe account and out to your bank on your normal schedule. It never sits in a LiveQue balance, because there isn't one.

What it costs

LiveQue takes nothing.

Not a percentage, not a monthly fee. The only thing that comes out is Stripe's card processing, which is the same wherever you take a card.

 Someone tips you$5.00
−Stripe card processing2.9% + 30¢, same as anywhere you take a card$0.45
−LiveQue's cut$0.00
=Lands in your bank$4.55

Smaller tips lose proportionally more to that flat 30¢ — a $2 tip nets $1.64. Worth knowing when you set your tip amounts. We'd rather you hear it from us than find it on a statement.
